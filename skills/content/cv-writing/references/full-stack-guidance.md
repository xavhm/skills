# Full-stack keyword selection and final review

Adapted from the article text supplied by the user, attributed in SKILL.md. Read this when selecting full-stack skills or checking a full-stack draft. The catalog preserves the source's technology coverage; it is a vocabulary aid, not a list to paste into every resume.

## How to select keywords

1. Read the posting, or define the role family for a standalone resume.
2. Mark relevant terms from the catalog and add relevant posting terms absent from it.
3. For each term, identify the candidate's actual experience. Omit unsupported terms; ask about meaningful unknowns.
4. Select approximately 15–25 supported skills overall, favoring direct relevance. A shorter truthful section is better than padded coverage.
5. Put three to four defining technologies in the summary, organize the skills section, and demonstrate important technologies in accomplishment bullets.

The source calls its tiers Must-Have, Should-Have, and Bonus. Here they are labeled **core vocabulary**, **role-dependent**, and **specialist**, because no candidate should claim a tool solely because the article calls it mandatory. The job's actual requirements determine priority.

## 12-point mistake check

| Check | Practical correction |
|---|---|
| 1. Technologies have no supporting context | Connect important tools to a specific contribution in Experience or Projects. |
| 2. Layout has multiple columns | Default to a single column with a clear reading order. If a user requires columns, test extraction and state its limits. |
| 3. Contact details exist only in a header/footer | Place essential identity and contact text in the document body. |
| 4. Skills inventory is excessive | Reduce toward 15–25 supported, relevant skills; avoid 40+ terms that crowd out proof. |
| 5. Irrelevant older technology dominates | Lead with the target stack; retain older tools when the job or accomplishment makes them relevant. |
| 6. Bullets describe generic responsibility | Replace the opening with what was actually built, changed, or delivered. |
| 7. Achievements have no evidence of result or scope | Seek verified business, technical, or scope measures; otherwise use a concrete qualitative outcome. Never fabricate a number. |
| 8. The application uses an untailored general resume | Apply Workflow B to title/positioning, skills, bullet priority, and project selection. |
| 9. Graphics carry essential content | Use ordinary text and a simple DOCX or text-based PDF. |
| 10. Skills have bars, stars, or percentages | Remove the ratings; prove capability through specific work. |
| 11. Target role is unclear | Use truthful target-role wording in the headline or summary while preserving historical titles. |
| 12. The strongest evidence is buried | Surface the most relevant achievement in the summary and/or first bullet of the appropriate role. |

## Source qualifications and editorial decisions

Retain the source's concrete writing recommendations without turning its generalizations into asserted facts:

- **Summary:** The article says three to four sentences, at most four. The user additionally prefers a maximum of four rendered lines. Apply both through compact wording, rather than confusing lines with sentences.
- **Keywords:** Keep the article's 15–25 skill target. Its 70% overlap suggestion is not a pass requirement; prioritize all important supported requirements without padding to reach a percentage.
- **ATS behavior:** Parsing, search, matching, and configured filtering are distinct capabilities. Do not assert that every ATS matches only literally, ignores every header, scrambles every column, or automatically rejects below 60–75%. Verify current official documentation if asked about a particular system.
- **Application outcomes:** Do not repeat the source's claimed 40% versus 80% matches, doubled pass rates, or assurances that 10–15 minutes of tailoring will produce interviews. Use the tailoring workflow because it improves relevance, not because of an unverified prediction.
- **File format:** Retain DOCX as a practical default when no format is specified; do not declare it superior across every ATS. Follow the portal and inspect the actual exported file.
- **Metrics:** Treat metrics as a drafting goal. Evidence can also be specific scope or an observable qualitative outcome. Never turn an example number into a candidate fact.
- **Seniority:** The source's years ranges help choose examples, but do not confer seniority or imply that every senior engineer manages people.
- **Credentials and older tools:** Check actual credential validity and job relevance; neither has a universal three-year deletion rule.
- **Technical examples:** Keep API latency distinct from browser Lighthouse scores. Describe Next.js rendering accurately instead of automatically labeling every application both an SPA and server-rendered. Claim authentication/SSO architecture only at the level supported by the candidate's evidence.
- **Source promotion:** Newsletter signup and product advertisements are omitted because they do not instruct resume writing. No purchase or account creation is needed for this workflow.
