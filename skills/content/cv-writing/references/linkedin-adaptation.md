# Resume-to-LinkedIn adaptation guide

Use with Workflow C. The foundation is the LinkedIn post supplied directly by the user, beginning “Resume vs. LinkedIn: what's the difference?” No author or source URL was supplied. Its core distinctions are translated into practical instructions below. The section templates and drafting targets also draw on the user's existing LinkedIn Profile Optimizer skill; this guide is self-contained.

## Change the context before changing the wording

A resume supports a particular selection decision. LinkedIn is a potentially broader professional introduction for recruiters, colleagues, candidates, clients, and partners. Do not simply paste the resume into a longer field. Decide what these readers should understand and what useful next step they could take.

| Dimension | Resume approach | LinkedIn adaptation |
|---|---|---|
| Audience | A particular hiring process or role family | A wider, potentially public professional audience; choose a primary reader |
| Purpose | Demonstrate suitability for a job | May support job search, hiring, selling, partnerships, reputation, or networking |
| Positioning | Select evidence for the application | Explain a coherent professional identity that remains useful across relevant opportunities |
| Content | Compress to the strongest selection evidence | Add understandable context, professional perspective, and proof where useful |
| Voice | Compact achievement statements | More conversational About text, with concise accomplishment bullets in Experience |
| Presentation | Clean document with predictable reading order | Scannable text plus appropriate photography, branding, and work samples |
| Maintenance | Tailor and refresh for applications; keep career records accurate | Review after meaningful professional changes, including outside a job search |
| Shared requirement | Accurate, relevant, readable, persuasive | Preserve the same facts and guide choices by what the reader needs to know |

The post's audience comparison describes potential reach, not a guarantee that a particular profile gets more views. Its visual advice is permission to use the medium well, not a requirement for emojis or logos. A resume can still benefit from being kept current outside an active search.

## 1. Choose the audience and select evidence

Write a one-sentence working brief: “Help [reader] understand [professional value], supported by [evidence], so they can [appropriate next step].” Do not publish this formula unchanged.

| Primary purpose | Prioritize | Possible closing invitation, only if wanted |
|---|---|---|
| Job search | Role fit, recurring skills, relevant outcomes, scope | Discuss opportunities in the stated role family |
| Hiring | Actual team remit, engineering problems, working practices, leadership evidence | Learn about verified openings or discuss the team's work |
| Selling or consulting | Actual offering, customer problem, delivery evidence | Discuss the relevant problem or service |
| Partnerships | Complementary capabilities, shared audiences, collaboration evidence | Explore a specific kind of collaboration |
| Professional reputation/networking | Specialty, useful perspective, public work, topics of interest | Connect around a shared professional interest |

Do not announce that the user is hiring, consulting, or seeking work unless they have stated that goal and the underlying claim is accurate. If several purposes apply, lead with one and support the others through evidence or the closing invitation.

## 2. Convert each section

These are editorial starting targets, not LinkedIn platform limits. Verify current official guidance or the live interface before asserting field limits, image dimensions, feature availability, or exact interface instructions. Do not infer search-ranking benefits from a keyword count.

### Headline

Draft one clear line using one of these patterns:

```text
[Professional role] | [Two or three defining specialties] | [Problem or audience]

[Professional role] helping [audience] with [supported capability]
```

Lead with recognizable professional identity. Retain a few central keywords, but broaden away from a single vacancy's wording when needed. Avoid a string of unrelated titles or a headline that says only “seeking opportunities.” A credible target role may describe direction; do not imply it is the current employment title when it is not.

### About

Start with roughly 150–250 words for a full draft; use less when the evidence or request is narrower. Use three to five short paragraphs, optionally replacing a paragraph with two or three proof bullets. This is a working writing target, not a requirement to fill space.

Draft in this order:

1. **Opening:** In the first two sentences, say what the person does, for whom, and why it matters. Do not start with a long chronology.
2. **Context:** Explain the professional thread connecting relevant roles or projects. Add motivation or perspective only when supplied or confirmed.
3. **Proof:** Include two or three supported accomplishments or concrete capabilities, ideally with relevant results.
4. **Focus:** State current specialties and direction naturally, without repeating the Skills inventory.
5. **Invitation:** Add one audience-appropriate next step when wanted. An invitation is optional; do not invent availability or publish contact details from the resume automatically.

Use first person by default. Do not force the resume's four-line summary limit onto About. Remove generic self-praise and avoid turning the field into an autobiography.

### Experience

Keep factual employment fields aligned with the resume. For each recent relevant role, use one or two sentences of context followed by three to five achievement bullets. Older roles may need less.

```text
[Actual title] — [Employer]
[Accurate dates]

[What the product/team does, who it serves, and the candidate's remit.]

• [Contribution + useful context + verified outcome]
• [Contribution + useful context + verified outcome]
• [Additional evidence relevant to the wider professional audience]
```

Explain an unfamiliar company or internal project in plain language. Expand acronyms when a mixed audience needs them. Preserve technical detail that proves capability, while making the benefit understandable. Restore relevant work omitted from a narrowly tailored resume only when evidence is available. Keep team and individual contribution distinct.

### Skills

Create a prioritized list of supported skills relevant to the professional positioning. Start by identifying the five most central skills, then add useful supporting capabilities. This prioritization is an editorial choice, not a claim about a platform ranking formula.

Use the resume as evidence but reconsider its order and omissions for the broader goal. Do not enforce the resume's 15–25 range as a LinkedIn platform requirement or fill every available slot. Place terms naturally in Headline, About, Experience, and Skills where they help understanding.

### Projects, education, and credentials

Preserve names, status, dates, and qualifications. Expand project context when it demonstrates relevant capability, and link to a supplied public artifact when appropriate. Include learning context for a career transition without presenting study as commercial delivery. Do not silently upgrade a degree or certification while rephrasing it.

### Featured and visual presentation

Suggest two to four strong work samples when available: a public demo, repository, case study, presentation, article, or product walkthrough. For each, specify the actual artifact, a clear display title, and a one-sentence explanation of what it proves. If no suitable artifact exists, suggest a concrete future item as a recommendation, not as an existing achievement.

Recommend a current, clear profile photo and a banner that supports the professional focus. A product image, appropriate work photograph, or simple positioning graphic may be useful. Use logos only when affiliation and permission are appropriate; do not invent brand associations.

Use whitespace and ordinary bullets for scanning. Emojis are optional accents: if they fit the user's voice, try one consistent marker for a short list or section label. Avoid emoji-heavy headlines, decorative Unicode alphabets, and symbols replacing essential words. Do not produce images or change branding unless requested.

## 3. Worked transformation

Fictional demonstration; these facts must never be transferred to the user's profile without evidence.

**Source evidence:** A developer used React, Node.js, and PostgreSQL to build an internal reporting dashboard. Operations used it instead of manually combining exports. No time saving was measured. The developer wants to connect with engineering teams working on internal products.

**Resume bullet:** Built an internal reporting dashboard with React, Node.js, and PostgreSQL, replacing manual export consolidation for operations.

**LinkedIn headline:** Full-Stack Developer | React, Node.js & PostgreSQL | Internal Tools

**About excerpt:** I build internal tools that make operational work easier. One example is a reporting dashboard I developed with React, Node.js, and PostgreSQL, which replaced the operations team's manual process of combining exports. I'm interested in connecting with engineering teams working on internal products.

**Experience context:** Developed an internal reporting tool for the operations team, covering the interface, service layer, and database queries.

**Featured recommendation:** A short public walkthrough could illustrate the workflow and engineering choices if an approved version can be shared. Do not assume internal screenshots or a private repository can be published.

The profile adds audience, context, and an appropriate invitation. It retains the same technology and outcome facts and does not invent time saved, user counts, ownership scope, or a passion statement.

## 4. Final checks and delivery

Check these before returning copy:

- Can the intended reader identify the person's work, relevance, and strongest proof from the opening?
- Does the profile remain useful beyond one vacancy without becoming vague?
- Do titles, dates, employers, qualifications, and metrics agree with the resume?
- Are personal viewpoints, public job-search signals, services, and hiring claims supported by the user's stated intentions?
- Is each section scannable with short paragraphs and useful bullets?
- Do visuals and links add evidence or context rather than clutter?
- Are suggested public details suitable for sharing, especially client names, internal metrics, work samples, and contact information?
- Have exact platform constraints been checked if the response claims compliance with them?

Deliver ready-to-paste Headline, About, and requested Experience entries first. Follow with prioritized Skills and concrete Featured/visual recommendations when in scope. Put fact questions and missing assets separately; do not hide placeholders in finished copy. State whether an actual profile was inspected or the draft was based only on supplied material.

Recommend updating the profile after a role change, substantial achievement, new public work, or changed professional focus. No automatic publishing, reminders, profile edits, or outreach follow from a request to draft profile content.
