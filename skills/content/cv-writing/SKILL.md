# CV writing

Deliver finished copy or documents, using only the sections relevant to the request. Job-offer analysis can also be a standalone deliverable.

## Shared rules

- Read supplied material first. Establish target role, seniority, language/market, and output format; for LinkedIn, establish the audience and professional goal.
- Ask only for consequential missing facts, in a focused batch. Continue with supported material; keep unresolved questions outside finished copy.
- Never invent titles, tenure, skills, metrics, motivations, credentials, or links. Distinguish personal contribution from team results and paid work from academic/personal projects.
- Preserve factual consistency across documents. Keep historical titles accurate; clarify obscure internal titles with a truthful parenthetical equivalent when useful.
- Treat length/count targets as editorial defaults. Follow user preferences and application instructions first.

## 1. Resume structure

**Experienced:** Contact → Summary → Skills → Experience → Selected Projects if useful → Education/Certifications. Move Skills below Experience when accomplishments provide stronger evidence.

**Graduate or changer:** Contact → Summary → Skills → Relevant Projects/Experience → Education → Other Experience as useful. Move Education higher if it is the strongest qualification.

- **Contact:** Name, location, email, phone, and relevant supplied LinkedIn/portfolio/GitHub links. Keep essential details in the document body; omit unnecessary personal information.
- **Summary:** About three short sentences, at most four rendered lines. State accurate professional positioning, relevant scope, and one or two supported achievements. Engineers may name a few defining technologies; product managers should foreground product/customer scope and outcomes. Avoid generic self-praise and objectives.
- **Skills:** Group supported capabilities by meaningful categories. Omit empty groups, proficiency bars, and personality labels. Aim for roughly 15–25 relevant skills when justified, rather than padding to a quota.
- **Experience:** Reverse chronology; title, employer, consistent dates. Usually three to five bullets for recent relevant roles, one or two for older work. Reorder bullets by relevance within each role, keeping employment chronological.
- **Projects:** Include projects that add evidence. Give name, context, stack/methods, two or three contribution/result bullets, and a supplied public link if available.
- **Education/credentials:** Exact qualification, institution/issuer, completion date or ongoing status. Compress once experience carries the application; retain coursework only when relevant. Verify validity before describing a certification as current.
- **Length:** Start with one page for early careers; use two when substantial relevant evidence warrants it. Remove repetition before shrinking type. Use readable 10–12 pt body text and margins of at least 0.5 inch / 1.3 cm.

Select evidence for the target role:

| Role | Evidence to prioritize when supported |
|---|---|
| Software engineering | System design and tradeoffs, implementation, testing, reliability, performance, maintainability, delivery; technical leadership appropriate to seniority. |
| Web/full-stack engineering | Coherent user workflows connecting interface, APIs, data, and deployment; name only layers personally handled. |
| Frontend engineering | UI delivery, accessibility, responsive behavior, browser performance, design systems, testing, and collaboration with design/product. Distinguish browser metrics from backend latency. |
| Product management | Discovery, problem framing, prioritization and tradeoffs, roadmap decisions, experiments, launches, stakeholder alignment, and adoption/revenue/retention outcomes. Show decisions and results beyond ceremonies and feature lists. |

## 2. Resume ATS compatibility and relevance

- Follow the portal's file instructions. Otherwise use a simple DOCX or text-based PDF; no format works best for every system.
- Default to a single column, standard headings such as Experience, Skills, and Education, ordinary bullets, and consistent dates. Avoid essential content in tables, text boxes, images, headers, or footers.
- Use recognizable role/skill terminology. Include the posting's exact phrase when it truthfully describes the candidate's work. Pair acronyms with expanded forms where useful; avoid listing aliases repeatedly.
- Put central terms naturally in Summary and Skills, then demonstrate important capabilities in Experience/Projects. Optimize relevance and evidence rather than repetition.
- Never use hidden keywords, copied requirement blocks, unsupported skills, keyword-density targets, fabricated match scores, or guaranteed pass rates. Missing wording and missing qualifications require different actions.
- For file output, extract text and inspect reading order, contacts, headings, dates, and bullets; render the file to check page breaks, clipping, and spacing. Text extraction is a basic compatibility check, not proof of an ATS result. Do not claim file checks for plain-text copy.

## 3. Resume bullet optimization

Use **action + specific deliverable/problem + relevant method + result or scope**. Keep one main achievement per bullet, usually one or two rendered lines.

- Replace “responsible for,” “worked on,” and “helped with” with the actual contribution. Use “led” only when leadership is supported.
- Lead with the accomplishment most relevant to the target. Name tools/methods when they explain delivery, rather than appending a technology inventory.
- Prefer measured outcomes with units, baselines, and periods when known. Distinguish percentage points from relative percentages, tests from production, and contribution from sole causation.
- If impact is unmeasured, use verified scope or a concrete qualitative result. Ask about missing measurements; never manufacture them.
- Show collaboration through actual decisions or delivery. Attribute team results accurately, using “contributed to” when appropriate.
- For product management, connect discovery/prioritization decisions to user or business outcomes; shipping alone does not prove impact.

**Illustrative rewrite, only when supported:** “Worked on dashboards” → “Built a React reporting page backed by a Node.js API and PostgreSQL queries, replacing manual CSV exports for operations.” Do not add time savings if none were measured.

For numerical claims: “Reduced [measure] from [baseline] to [result] by [intervention].” Remove unsupported clauses; template slots are never candidate facts.

## 4. Cover-letter rules

- For targeted letters, select two or three central job needs and one or two matching examples. For general-purpose letters, choose a coherent role family without inventing an employer or vacancy.
- Default to one page, three to five paragraphs, roughly 250–400 words; write less when the application or evidence calls for it.
- **Opening:** Identify the role, background, and strongest reason to consider the candidate. Avoid an opening consisting only of “I am writing to apply.”
- **Body:** Develop examples as brief context/problem → personal action → verified result → relevance to the role. Add useful context instead of repeating the resume or listing every skill.
- **Employer connection:** Tie a specific product, audience, responsibility, or priority to actual experience or confirmed motivation. Use supplied facts or verify new current claims through official employer sources. Avoid generic praise and invented enthusiasm.
- **Close:** Reinforce contribution, invite discussion, and thank the reader. Introduce no new claims.
- Use natural first-person prose. Match the resume's header/font for a document; use a verified recipient or “Dear Hiring Manager.” Omit unknown address fields instead of guessing.
- Check employer/role names, facts, repetition, placeholders, and page fit. Tailoring a reusable base requires revisiting the opening, employer connection, and evidence selection.

## 5. LinkedIn profile structure

Optimize for a coherent role family and audience beyond one vacancy. Preserve resume facts while adding understandable context.

1. **Headline:** Recognizable professional identity, defining specialties, and useful product/domain focus.
2. **About / Info:** What the person does, whom it helps, evidence, and current professional focus.
3. **Experience:** Actual titles/dates; one or two sentences on product/team context and remit, followed by three to five achievement bullets for recent roles. Compress older entries.
4. **Skills:** Prioritize central supported capabilities, then supporting skills; align with positioning and demonstrate them in Experience.
5. **Featured / Projects:** Select a few public demos, repositories, case studies, articles, or presentations; explain what each proves. Never assume private work can be published.
6. **Education / Credentials:** Accurate qualifications/status. Suggest a clear photo and relevant banner when visual advice is in scope.

## 6. LinkedIn headline and About discoverability

### Headline

- Lead with a recognizable role term; add two or three supported specialties and, if useful, product/domain focus.
- Pattern: **[Role] | [Key specialties] | [Product/domain or audience]**. Frontend specialties might include accessibility and design systems; PM specialties might include B2B SaaS and product discovery, only when supported.
- Use terms recurring across relevant offers rather than unrelated titles/stacks. Avoid slogans, decorative Unicode, and a headline consisting only of “seeking opportunities.”
- Describe career direction honestly without implying a current position or seniority the person does not hold.

### About / Info

- Start with roughly 150–250 words in three to five short paragraphs; do not pad sparse evidence.
- In the first two sentences, identify professional focus, audience/product, and value. Follow with the career thread and two or three concrete accomplishments or capabilities.
- Weave role terms, specialties, and domain vocabulary naturally into the explanation; reinforce them through Skills and Experience. Avoid keyword lists masquerading as biography.
- Use first person and plain language. Add motivation or perspective only when known; avoid autobiography and unsupported adjectives.
- Close with current direction and an optional audience-appropriate invitation. Do not announce job seeking, consulting, hiring, or availability without the user's intent, or automatically publish resume contact details.
- Treat wording as relevance and clarity work; do not promise search ranking. Verify current platform limits/interface details when needed; editorial targets are not platform limits.

## 7. Analyze a job offer for keywords and wording

Read the full posting, retrieving a supplied link when needed. Extract responsibilities as well as technology nouns; do not infer requirements from the title alone.

1. Identify exact title, seniority/scope, required versus preferred criteria, domain/product, expected outcomes, and explicit eligibility constraints.
2. Group terms into role identity, skills/tools, methods, responsibilities, domain, and outcomes. Prioritize explicit requirements and central responsibilities; repetition is a signal, not a scoring formula.
3. Map important terms to candidate evidence:

   | Posting term/formulation | Required / preferred / responsibility | Candidate evidence | Action |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Exact wording | Priority from posting | Specific role/project, adjacent evidence, unknown, or confirmed gap | Retain, rephrase, ask, or leave unclaimed |

4. Distinguish direct from transferable experience. Do not substitute React for Vue, one cloud for another, or participation for ownership. For PM roles, extract discovery, prioritization, experimentation, stakeholder, and product-outcome language when present.
5. Recommend placements: truthful target wording in headline/summary, supported tools in Skills, responsibility phrases grounded in accomplishments. Reorder evidence without changing facts.
6. Deliver prioritized keywords, supported rewrite suggestions, and material gaps. Without candidate evidence, deliver posting analysis and evidence questions; do not claim candidate fit.

## Final pass

Check role relevance, factual consistency, natural wording, and requested format. Remove unsupported claims and drafting placeholders. Return the requested artifact first, with concise unresolved questions separately. Preserve the source resume when tailoring unless replacement was requested. Drafting does not authorize profile publication, application submission, or outreach.
